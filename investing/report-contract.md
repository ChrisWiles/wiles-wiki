# Weekly reports for decisions

Long-term AI infrastructure ownership is the core objective. Swing trading is a supplement with no approved numerical sleeve or per-trade risk budget. Reports should make decisions and unresolved questions easy to find without pretending incomplete analysis is a recommendation.

## Research report

Open with the thesis verdict and what changed since the prior distinct report. Explain which evidence changes could affect a long-term decision, while distinguishing capability growth, economic demand, compute efficiency and supplier/operator economics. Include the strongest disconfirming evidence. Link consequential scientific questions to R-IDs in the research queue. Show verified scope and remaining source backlog; a committed partial report is not completed coverage.

## Stock report

Start with a compact decision brief. For each material proposal, state the security/account scope, long-term versus swing horizon, analytical stance, evidence and date, valuation/price condition if supportable, invalidation condition, key blocker and next action. Appropriate stances include consider add, hold after review, consider reduce/exit, wait, research first, or insufficient evidence. These are analytical proposals, not executed trades. Unassessed companies cannot be labeled hold/buy merely to fill the brief.

Then include:
1. **Core portfolio decisions:** material thesis/economics/valuation changes and what would change the conclusion. Separate business quality from today's price.
2. **Exposure:** account and supported household/ETF look-through findings, with known-subset denominators and missing quantities clear. Household investment data is authorized for publication.
3. **Prioritized next research:** normally up to three active questions from the queue, why now, decision affected, completion criterion, owner/trigger and R-ID. Show important awaiting-user inputs separately so they do not stop independent work.
4. **Decision changes and follow-up:** proposed recommendations, user-confirmed choices, verified executions and due journal reviews remain distinct. Link D-IDs; unchanged weeks do not manufacture decisions.
5. **Supplementary swing watch:** conditional setups with reliable data, invalidation and event checks, or a no-trade conclusion. Missing risk inputs prevent sizing; tactical moves alone do not change the core thesis.
6. **Coverage and next events:** brief check of every symbol, deep-dive rotation, source dates/gaps and verified event triggers. A due-date guess is not a sourced earnings calendar.

Limit emphasis to developments that could change a decision. Preserve prior dated reports; on same-date reruns compare to the prior distinct date. Evidence-backed 'wait' is a useful result; a data gap should produce 'research first/insufficient evidence', not a manufactured investment judgment.

## Journal and learning

Capture original reasoning before outcomes are known, with a minimal material-evidence snapshot and immutable committed report links where available. Same-date report revisions must leave the original decision basis inspectable. Evaluate process, thesis observations and actual outcomes separately. Returns require verified execution and appropriate measurement. Use review triggers and stable review identity rather than logging identical entries every week. Repeating a run with identical evidence creates neither a second decision/review nor a journal-only commit. Recover retained unpublished proposals before assigning new IDs after publication failure. Record skill-improvement ideas only when a concrete workflow failure is repeatable and the proposed change has an observable acceptance criterion.
