# AGI scenario method

This is a thesis-testing sensitivity model, alongside the unchanged historical market model. It connects the user's **2027–2029 capability scenario** to adoption, useful paid workloads, computing efficiency and per-share economics. It is not a completed valuation of every issuer.

The September 2026 [Anthropic economic scenarios](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios) separate technical capability from adoption. The [technical report](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cf58f84d46a4a76bf5a5b039ac695fba6b80041c.pdf), particularly its task framework and capital-supply sections, explains why capital adjustment affects who captures gains. These are economic scenarios, without assigned stock probabilities. Anthropic does not provide our dates, workloads, stock assumptions or weights. Its RSI research supports distinguishing assistance from autonomous successor development. The [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) supplies an independent physical/financing cross-check. Research checked October 17, 2026; no new complete weekly source sweep is implied.

## Calculation

Every held ticker is assigned exactly once to an economic group. Quantity times the frozen closing price establishes starting wealth. The two Alphabet listings share factors; the two Nasdaq-100 funds share factors. Funds use aggregate earnings-capacity proxies, without guessed constituent weights. Other businesses retain non-AI drivers.

At each six-month/one- through five-year horizon:

1. Adoption starts at the branch's capability date plus a separate calendar-month lag. Before then, the incremental transformative-AI demand index is one. Ordinary business growth can continue.
2. Paid workload index = annual workload multiplier raised to years since adoption. Compute per workload = annual efficiency multiplier raised to the negative of those years. Physical compute = their product. This is not an electricity forecast: energy efficiency, utilization and cooling are additional variables.
3. Revenue index = `(1 + ordinary revenue growth)^years × demand index^elasticity`. Equipment/operators use physical compute, platforms/funds use paid workloads, and non-AI holdings have no incremental demand driver. Elasticities are analyst settings, not measured market shares or company guidance.
4. Cash-earning-capacity index = revenue index × cash-conversion ratio. The terminal cash-conversion ratio ramps geometrically from one over five years. It combines operating margin, replacement/growth investment and financing burden; it is **not an observed FCF margin**. Operators additionally have explicit annual share dilution. This is intentionally simpler than a debt/FCF model.
5. Price factor = cash-earning-capacity index ÷ diluted-share index × valuation-multiple ratio. The terminal multiple ratio reprices smoothly toward its five-year assumption with a 1.2-year exponential time constant, normalized to reach the terminal ratio at five years. Repricing can happen before adoption because markets anticipate future economics. This is a chosen repricing convention, not a simulated price path or evidence of market timing.
6. Multiply each recorded security's starting value by its factor; sum across securities and independently across accounts. No return from the historical model is added or multiplied in.

All operating growth, elasticity, conversion, dilution and valuation parameters are explicitly stored in `investing/models/agi-inputs/`. Starting normalization is one; it does not assert current positive owner earnings. For an unprofitable operator this approach cannot represent its actual negative cash flows, net debt or default mechanics. Those require a company valuation before using a result to recommend a purchase. Category assumptions hide differences within groups, including mature Oracle versus Nebius, NVIDIA versus Cerebras, and GE Vernova versus Vertiv. Their actual shares and starting marks remain distinct.

No separate dividend distribution or reinvestment is modeled. This is a **price-only** bridge; the historical model uses reinvested total returns. A comparison between them changes both method and income policy. There are no deposits, withdrawals, rebalancing, swing profits or option rolls. Options, cash, Treasury notes, borrowing, taxes, inflation and unrecorded assets are outside the model. These omissions do not establish zero balances.

## Scenarios and weights

Each branch describes a different joint world: early capability/durable capture; 2028–29 capability/measured adoption; capability with efficiency/substitution dominating hardware; early capability/overbuild and financing stress; or delayed capability. Branches are exclusive **modeling cases**, rather than a proven exhaustive partition of reality. The capability milestone is an explicit convention for dependable multiday cognitive and AI-research work, not proof of AGI or sustained RSI. Dates within the user's window are analyst choices.

Working weights are 20%, 35%, 15%, 10% and 20%, respectively. They are provisional analyst judgments with no statistical calibration or user endorsement. Measured adoption receives the largest weight; other branches prevent equating earlier capability with higher returns. The 80% weight placed inside 2027–2029 reflects this chosen exercise, **not a sourced estimate that AGI has an 80% chance of arriving**. No source identifies these exact weights. Equal weights and a more cautious allocation test dependence on this judgment. Neither alternative is a confidence bound.

A weighted endpoint is an arithmetic weighted average, not a median. The minimum/maximum of five deterministic endpoints is not a confidence interval or possible-outcome boundary. The sum of weights on endpoints below the starting value or above twice it is only an endpoint classification; it is not a calibrated loss/doubling probability. Do not present those fields as investor odds. Within-branch market risk, permanent losses, policy shocks and catastrophic tails remain unmodeled. The original market-model odds retain their separate, limited validation.

## Reproduction, review and history

Run `agi_scenarios.py --input investing/models/agi-inputs/YYYY-MM-DD-vN.json --output investing/forecasts/A-YYYY-MM-DD-vN.json` using the portfolio-manager scripts. Run `test_agi_scenarios.py`; reconcile starting wealth, six targets, all securities/accounts, aliases, weight sums and independent bridge arithmetic. Check the frozen baseline/holdings/input hashes. Published A/P files and their input vintages are immutable. Same-date corrections use a new version with a written reason. A stock report names its exact A-ID with `<!-- agi-scenario-id: A-YYYY-MM-DD-vN -->`. Older editions receive no backfilled scenarios.

Weekly work reviews the **evidence and assumptions**, rather than manufacturing a weekly probability change. Check source dates/methods, reliability, paid adoption, workload/efficiency balance, orders/margins/cash, financing, dilution and valuations. Retain unchanged parameters with their original date and rationale. If operating assumptions, weights or scope change, publish a new immutable input/result and explain the changed judgment separately from market performance. If evidence is stale or insufficient, carry the older vintage explicitly or omit the new layer; do not silently relabel it current. Do not change model or renderer code during a recurring run.

The next upgrades are issuer-specific normalized cash flow, verified fund look-through and sensitivity to adoption lags, operating conversion and exit valuations. Weight sensitivity alone does not validate the branch endpoints. At a due target, compare the original endpoint with a supported fixed-holdings realized price value using the last completed US session on or before the target. Record large misses and assumption failures without calling deterministic endpoints calibrated probability forecasts. Actual household performance requires transactions and cash-flow reconciliation.
