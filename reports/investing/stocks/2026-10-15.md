# Stock and household portfolio review — October 15, 2026

My recommendations from this review are to **wait before buying more Nebius or Oracle, hold Vertiv for now without adding, and simplify future Nasdaq-100 purchases if that exposure still fits our goals.** We need to check tax lots before considering sales of existing holdings. I also want a clearer picture of Alphabet's underlying earnings before adding to it.

These are proposals; this review records no executed trades or user acceptance of them. The prices used are the October 14 closing prices; the research includes the October 15 morning updates.

## Our biggest portfolio question is overlap

QQQ and QQQM together represent **68.28% of the stocks and ETFs recorded in our two Robinhood accounts**. That calculation excludes E*TRADE, cash, options and Treasury notes, so it is not a percentage of our entire household portfolio. It does show that the investments we can measure already have substantial Nasdaq-100 exposure.

Both funds give us exposure to the same index. We also own individual companies that can overlap with the funds. Before adding more, I want to measure how much we own of each company once those layers are combined.

If we were already planning another long-term Nasdaq-100 purchase and that exposure still fits our goals, I would use one preferred fund, with QQQM the candidate. Invesco describes QQQM as the lower-expense buy-and-hold choice and QQQ as the more trading-focused choice. That does not justify selling appreciated QQQ just to tidy up the account. We need the purchase lots and tax impact before considering that. [Invesco's comparison][QQQ].

## Why I would wait on Nebius and Oracle

Both companies have evidence of demand. My concern is how much money their expansion needs and what shareholders keep after financing costs and equipment replacement.

Nebius spent substantially more buying equipment and other assets in the second quarter than it brought in from operations. Its projected payback on new contracts also includes forecasts and capacity that has not yet been built. Oracle has strong cloud growth, but part of its operating cash came from customers paying in advance, and it has substantial future data-center lease commitments. The figures and sources are in the financial detail below.

I would wait before adding to either position until we can explain how the buildout is funded, how new financing affects existing shareholders, and whether customers pay enough to cover the equipment over its useful life. Better evidence on those points could reopen an addition. Funding problems, weaker rental prices or cancellations could instead lead to a review of whether to reduce the holdings. This is not a sell recommendation or a position-size instruction.

## Why I would keep Vertiv under review

Vertiv's second-quarter margins and cash generation support continuing to own it after this limited review. I would hold the recorded shares for now and wait on additions. A falling share price alone does not make a purchase attractive.

The next useful evidence is whether orders turn into sales and cash while margins hold up. Guidance cuts, cancellations or weaker results would make me revisit the recommendation. GE Vernova also has orders supporting the infrastructure thesis, but I have not finished the valuation work needed for a new allocation.

## A few findings deserve closer attention

**Alphabet's reported profit needs unpacking.** Its cloud business grew strongly, but reported earnings also include a large gain on investments. We need to separate that from the money the business earns through its operations. Direct Alphabet holdings already account for 23.28% of Chris's recorded Robinhood stocks and ETFs, before counting the funds' Alphabet exposure. [Filed earnings release][GOOG].

**Micron and Sandisk need a consistent earnings comparison.** The earnings figures in the quote feed differ from the latest fiscal-year releases. Their profits also need to be assessed across the memory cycle. A low-looking price-to-earnings ratio does not settle whether either stock is cheap.

**TSMC and Rocket Lab have fresh developments.** TSMC's October 15 results support the chip-manufacturing case, although the prices in this report predate that announcement. Rocket Lab said on October 14 that Neutron's first launch is now expected in 2027. We need to revisit the original reason for owning Rocket Lab and how long its cash will last. [TSMC results][TSM], [Rocket Lab update][RKLB].

## Swing trades and taxes

I am not recommending a swing trade to act on from this review. The selected charts do not establish a validated reversal or breakout, event checks remain incomplete, and we have not set the trading-risk limits or supplied the complete cash and account values needed to size a trade.

All three accounts are taxable, but we still lack the purchase dates, adjusted costs and transaction history. That prevents a reliable calculation of sale taxes, long-term holding dates or possible wash sales across our accounts. The simplification recommendation concerns future purchases; it does not call for selling existing holdings for tax reasons.

2. **Alphabet's underlying earnings:** work out a valuation that separates investment gains from operating results and accounts for AI spending.
3. **Nebius and Oracle's funding:** compare customer payments, debt, new shares and equipment costs under a less favorable demand scenario.
