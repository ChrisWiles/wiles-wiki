# Portfolio projections — October 17, 2026

Our recorded stock portfolio could grow while still exposing us to large losses. The first conditional model puts the five-year median near **$5.53 million**, against **$4.20 million** today. It estimates a **35% chance of ending below today's value**, and a **28% chance of doubling**. These probabilities come from the assumptions below; they are not measured real-world frequencies or an AGI forecast.

The point of this model is to make the tradeoff concrete, not to manufacture a precise target. Over five years, the model gives an **85% chance of a 30% peak-to-trough fall**, even though most simulated outcomes finish ahead. Monthly observations can miss deeper falls between month ends.

## What is included

All 28 recorded stocks/ETFs across Chris E*TRADE, Chris Robinhood and Jen Robinhood, priced at October 16 closes: **$4,200,145.05**. The current quantities are frozen for this forecast, with dividends reinvested into the same security. No new deposits, withdrawals, cross-security rebalancing, future stock-picking profits or option rolls are assumed. Fund expenses are embedded once in the return history.

Excluded, not treated as zero: options, all cash, Treasury notes, borrowing, unrecorded assets, taxes and inflation. Robinhood position snapshots are October 14; E*TRADE is October 16. Missing tax lots do not prevent this pre-tax stock model. Verified option/cash inputs would be needed to extend it to complete account forecasts. No personal risk tolerance or target allocation is inferred.

## How much the assumptions change the answer

These use the same holdings, starting value and random seed. They are separate conditional cases with no assigned scenario probabilities; they are not a weighted bull/base/bear forecast.

| Case | One-year median | One-year loss chance | Five-year median | Five-year loss chance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Main estimate | $4.57M | 39% | $5.53M | 35% |
| Lower market returns (-4pp) | $4.35M | 45% | $4.39M | 48% |
| Higher market returns (+4pp) | $4.79M | 34% | $6.99M | 24% |
| 25% larger shocks | $4.46M | 44% | $4.83M | 43% |
| More correlated company shocks | $4.50M | 42% | $5.33M | 37% |

A modest change in the assumed return can change the decision one might draw from the forecast. This is why the 35% five-year loss estimate should not be treated as a reliable single number. Uncertainty about the model matters in addition to the randomness inside it.

## Test the business thesis separately

These statistical probabilities do not complete the company valuations. A simple Alphabet cross-check shows the difference: after removing the Q2 after-tax equity-gain contribution, its quarter-repeat EPS illustration is $11.40. Five years of 0%, 10% or 20% EPS growth combined with assumed ending multiples of 20, 25 or 30 would imply roughly **$228, $459 or $851** per share, respectively, before dividends. Those assumptions are not forecasts or assigned probabilities; they illustrate how different operating/valuation combinations can dominate a price-history model. This is not a complete normalized cash-flow valuation. [Issuer EPS reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm).

For NVIDIA and the other suppliers, customer spending, architecture and margins still need underwriting. For operators, debt, dilution, rental prices and replacing equipment still matter. A price model cannot prove that these businesses generate the cash their valuations require. The following stress cases translate different thesis failures into explicit, unequal holding shocks.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Profitable deployment | $5.02M | +19% |
| Slower monetization | $3.03M | -28% |

The direct-NVIDIA permanent-loss case removes the direct shares entirely but omits NVIDIA losses inside funds and contagion; it understates a full issuer-failure scenario. The baseline has no calibrated bankruptcy hazard, and its lower percentile is not the worst possible outcome.

## What was validated — and what was not

The calculation tests cover starting-value conservation, no cash-flow injection, joint benchmark paths, reproducibility, negative-drift/permanent-loss behavior, training cutoffs, short-history proxies, covariance validity and quantile ordering. A second seed with 25,000 paths changed loss probabilities by at most **0.43 percentage points** and medians by at most **0.42%**, compared with the main 50,000-path run. More paths reduce sampling noise; they do not fix an incorrect model.

Historical-origin checks used data available at each annual January origin from 2010 onward, forecasting a 50/50 SPY/QQQ buy-and-hold proxy. No future return entered that origin's estimates. These are proxy diagnostics, not a backtest of our actual household selection. Current-vintage adjusted prices can still carry vendor revisions. Longer horizons overlap and provide few independent observations.

| Horizon | Mature proxy origins | Middle-80% range coverage | Loss-event Brier score | Simple Gaussian reference Brier | Model / reference interval score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Six months | 17 | 94% | 0.169 | 0.213 | 0.378 / 0.366 |
| One year | 16 | 100% | 0.198 | 0.219 | 0.548 / 0.530 |
| Two years | 15 | 80% | 0.155 | 0.171 | 0.995 / 0.942 |
| Three years | 14 | 86% | 0.152 | 0.167 | 1.365 / 1.319 |
| Four years | 13 | 85% | 0.151 | 0.164 | 1.638 / 1.973 |
| Five years | 12 | 58% | 0.153 | 0.167 | 2.739 / 3.593 |

Lower Brier and interval scores are better. A constant 50% loss prediction has a Brier score of 0.250. The model improves loss-event scores on this sample, but the five-year band covers only 7/12 cases, below its intended 80%. The model is not consistently better on interval score at shorter horizons either. **This is not demonstrated household calibration.** Do not tune assumptions to these outcomes and then call the same sample an independent validation; preserve this version and test future observations.

## Reproduce this forecast

Identity: `P-2026-10-16-v1`, model `factor-block-v1`. Starting marks October 16, 2026; complete monthly training returns February 2000–September 2026. The incomplete October bar and duplicated latest daily bar were excluded. Source: public Yahoo chart adjusted closes, interpreted as split/distribution-adjusted total returns. The adjustment history and every quote were not independently reconstructed.

- 50,000 consistent monthly joint paths, seed `20261017`, 60 months. Three-month circular blocks preserve some historical market/technology relationships and serial dependence.
- Two common factors: SPY return and QQQ-minus-SPY return. Company factor loadings and residual risk fit at most 60 completed months, at least 18. No historical stock-specific alpha or extra technology premium is extrapolated.
- Broad-market approximate arithmetic drift is **9.28% annually**, estimated from the long sample. The 3% reference rate is a model assumption, not a claim about today's Treasury yield. Neither is a six-month expert return forecast. Stock log drift deducts variance; historical-return uncertainty is drawn per path from 12-month blocks. Full uncertainty in betas/covariance is not modeled.
- Company residuals use correlated Gaussian shocks. Pairwise correlations with at least 12 shared months are shrunk 25% off-diagonal and made mathematically valid. Market blocks retain historical non-normality, but unseen events and company defaults are not calibrated. More correlated and larger shocks are separate sensitivity cases.
- CBRS has four complete monthly returns and uses NVIDIA risk parameters; SPCX has three and uses Rocket Lab parameters. Their residual noise is increased 25% above the larger peer/own short-sample estimate. These are uncertain parameter proxies, not invented pre-IPO prices or claims that the businesses are equivalent. NBIS history before September 2024 is excluded. Other short histories retain their observed periods. No older unrelated SPCX ticker is spliced in.

## How we will learn from it

Keep this forecast fixed. Its first target is April 16, 2027; the last is October 16, 2031. Future weekly reports may publish new vintages but must retain the old inputs, policy, target dates and assumptions. At maturity, score the original events and interval coverage/width. A year's overlapping weekly forecasts are not independent trials. Actual household results need verified transactions and cash flows and must be kept separate from this frozen-policy calculation.

The next model improvements are valuation-informed return assumptions and stronger independent long-horizon validation. Better data and financial underwriting can change the outlook. An automated headline-to-growth-rate adjustment cannot substitute for that work.

Method references, checked October 17: [CFA on simulation and backtesting](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/backtesting-and-simulation), [Gneiting/Raftery on proper scoring](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), and [Vanguard on hypothetical distributions and horizon limits](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/vemo-return-forecasts.html). These inform the design; this model does not reproduce those providers' methods or use Vanguard's ten-year expected return as a short-term forecast.

Target dates are calendar horizons. Score the last completed US trading-session close on or before each target date, including weekends/holidays; no trade is assumed on that date.
