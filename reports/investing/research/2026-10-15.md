# AI infrastructure research — October 15, 2026

The latest research gives us more reason to believe businesses will use a lot more AI. That supports the long-term case for the infrastructure behind it. The investment question is whether the companies we own can turn that demand into lasting profits at a price worth paying.

My view remains the same: keep testing the AI infrastructure thesis, and be selective about the businesses we own. AGI arriving in 2027–2029 remains a possibility. The evidence does not give us enough confidence to treat that timing as certain.

## What's new

**Businesses are doing more work with agents.** OpenAI's October 14 report says Codex and ChatGPT Work now account for more than half of the output tokens in its EU customer sample. Tokens are pieces of text generated by the models, so this measures activity rather than the share of jobs being automated. Weekly active agent users among small and medium businesses grew more than eightfold since May. That is encouraging evidence of use, although it comes from OpenAI's own customers and does not tell us how profitable that work is. [OpenAI's report](https://openai.com/index/the-eus-next-chapter-in-ai-is-agentic/).

**We still need better evidence about how much AI speeds up AI research.** On October 14, METR proposed a way to assess that, using information about models, computing resources, researchers and improvements to algorithms. It is a plan for measuring acceleration, not a completed independent assessment. [METR's proposal](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-14-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment-proposal/).

I also checked more of the older research behind our thesis. One experiment, AIDE², found that a research agent could improve its own software and carry some of those improvements into new tests. That is useful progress, but it does not show an AI independently creating and training its own more capable successor. Those distinctions matter when judging how quickly demand might grow. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26457).

## What this means for our investments

Power equipment, cooling, chip manufacturing, memory and networking remain important areas to investigate. Software can improve quickly, while factories, grid connections and data centers take time to build. That can create valuable business for suppliers, provided orders turn into profitable sales and cash.

For companies renting out computing capacity, I want to understand how much customers actually use, what they pay, and whether that covers financing and the eventual cost of replacing equipment. A growing backlog of contracts helps, but it does not answer all of those questions.

The [stock report](../stocks/2026-10-15.md) takes that work further. Our next priorities are the overlap in our existing Nasdaq-100 funds and individual stocks, Alphabet's earnings after removing investment gains, and the funding needs of Nebius and Oracle. Those questions are more likely to change a portfolio decision than another broad AGI prediction.

## The risk I would keep in view

AI could become much more useful while some infrastructure stocks disappoint. More efficient models and cheaper chips could reduce the computing resources needed for each task. Customers might also take longer to find profitable uses for all the capacity being built. Meanwhile, the companies supplying that capacity still have leases, equipment costs and financing commitments to meet.

I would become more confident if customers keep spending after prices fall, suppliers turn orders into cash without losing margins, and computing providers earn good returns after equipment replacement. Cancellations, idle capacity, weaker pricing or funding gaps would push me the other way.

## Appendix: how far the research claims go

The paper on recursive self-improvement, or RSI, tentatively estimates a 15% threshold against a rough observed 9%. Those figures describe AI research productivity per unit of the paper's capability measure. They depend on the model and its assumptions; they are not a universal measure of how much smarter an AI must become. I checked the introduction, while verification of the full technical model remains open. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15802).

Epoch's work on parallel research makes another useful point: adding more researchers or agents only helps if their work can be divided, coordinated and put together. Some experiments must happen in sequence. [Analysis](https://epoch.ai/publications/parallelization-constraints-could-delay-a-technological-singularity). DeepMind also reports that AlphaEvolve contributed to TPU chip design. That supports the possibility of AI improving its own infrastructure, while reminding us to account for efficiency gains and competing designs. [May 7 account](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-impact/).
