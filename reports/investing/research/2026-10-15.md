# AI infrastructure research — October 15, 2026

**Run:** manual execution of the weekly workflow, morning America/Chicago. **Comparison:** [October 14 baseline](2026-10-14.md). **Coverage:** primary-source refresh with remaining methods and search gaps; not an exhaustive literature review or an end-of-day market report.

**Assessment: the long-term infrastructure thesis remains supported, with stronger evidence of agent use and important limits on monetization and research acceleration.** Useful workloads can grow while efficiency reduces resources per task. Neither benchmark progress nor announced capacity establishes attractive shareholder returns. Retain the 2027–2029 AGI scenario without assigning it certainty.

## Sharper interpretation and the bear case

The RSI economics paper's tentative 15% threshold versus rough observed 9% concerns AI R&D productivity per unit of its capability measure. These are model-dependent calibration figures, not a universal percentage increase in intelligence or a countdown. The introduction was checked; full technical-model verification remains open. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15802).

Epoch's parallelization work emphasizes the tools needed to divide, execute and integrate research; sequential experiments can remain limiting. [Analysis](https://epoch.ai/publications/parallelization-constraints-could-delay-a-technological-singularity). DeepMind reports AlphaEvolve contributions to TPU circuit design, supporting infrastructure improvement while reinforcing efficiency/substitution risk. [May 7 account](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-impact/).

The strongest bear case is a mismatch: efficiency and cheaper architectures advance while profitable customer demand develops more slowly, but long-lived leases, power commitments and financing obligations remain. A correct technology thesis can coexist with poor investment returns.
