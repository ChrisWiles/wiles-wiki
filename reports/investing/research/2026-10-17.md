The case for a large AI infrastructure buildout remains intact. This review does not establish that AGI is imminent or that today's AI stocks are cheap. The most useful change since [Thursday's report](2026-10-15.md) is that we can now connect the research to a much fuller picture of our investments: the recorded stocks and funds total **$4.20 million**, with substantial direct NVIDIA and Alphabet exposure. **Keep studying who earns durable cash from the buildout before adding exposure.** Taiwan Semiconductor's latest guidance supports continued spending on advanced chips. The harder question is whether customers and computing operators earn enough to sustain that spending. More capable agents, more tokens and more infrastructure are useful evidence; none establishes attractive returns for shareholders by itself.

## What changed our view

**The physical spending cycle has another concrete checkpoint.** TSMC's October 15 results page gives fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $52.3–54.3 billion. Its call reiterates a $60–64 billion 2026 capital budget. These are management forecasts, not proof of future utilization or customer profitability. They support the foundry demand case while raising the importance of checking whether large investments ultimately pay for themselves. [Quarterly results](https://investor.tsmc.com/schinese/quarterly-results/2026/q3), [call transcript](https://investor.tsmc.com/japanese/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf).

**Safety and reliable deployment belong in our bottleneck analysis.** Anthropic's October 9 report, corrected October 12, describes agents taking unintended actions during testing and internal use. The company restricted live internet access across internal evaluations pending confirmation of safeguards. This is newly reviewed here, not a new incident this week. Our inference: supervision and containment can slow useful deployment even as capabilities improve. It does not demonstrate self-sustaining recursive improvement. [Anthropic's account and remediation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-unintended-model-actions).

**Research spending is still easier to observe than research output.** Epoch's October 5 Data Insight describes roughly monthly doubling in OpenAI researchers' coding-agent usage valued at API prices. Its October 16 entry describes a six-to-seven-month open/closed model gap on its cyber capability index. We verified these dated index summaries, not their underlying datasets or complete methods. Neither measures economy-wide paid demand or establishes an AGI date. [Epoch's dated entries](https://epoch.ai/data-insights).

METR's October 14 proposal remains particularly useful: it describes the internal data needed to attribute faster AI development to AI assistance. It is a proposed assessment, not a completed independent finding. The Research, Notes and Updates indexes were also checked; no newer completed R&D-acceleration assessment was identified in those retrieved listings. That is a limited search result, not proof that no relevant work exists. ## What this means for our holdings

Power equipment, cooling, memory and advanced manufacturing remain plausible constraints. The IEA summary still projects global data-center electricity use rising from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030 and expects HBM constraints through at least late 2027. These remain forecasts. The same summary warns that financing conditions can limit construction. A supplier can benefit from scarce equipment while its customer struggles to finance or operate a facility. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

For NVIDIA, Broadcom, TSMC and Micron, we need evidence that demand survives architectural changes and efficiency improvements. For Vertiv and GE Vernova, we need orders that turn into profitable deliveries and cash. For Nebius and Oracle, financing, customer payments and the cost of replacing equipment matter as much as sales growth. The stock review advances the Nebius funding check and Alphabet earnings review; it does not turn these research findings into automatic purchases.

The central test remains:

**Total compute needed = useful workloads × compute per workload.**

Electricity also depends on energy efficiency, utilization and cooling overhead. Cheaper API prices can reflect competition or subsidies as well as efficiency. We should not convert a price decline directly into an electricity forecast.

The strongest bear case is a mismatch in timing: customers adopt slowly, efficiency improves quickly, equipment arrives before profitable workloads, and operators still owe financing and replacement costs. Security restrictions or power delays can widen that mismatch. A more capable AI industry could coexist with disappointing returns on some of its infrastructure.

## What would change the conclusion

We would become more constructive on additions if recurring paid workloads, customer retention and utilization improve while suppliers retain margins and operators finance expansion without eroding per-share economics. We would weaken the infrastructure thesis if cancellations, idle capacity and falling rental prices persist while capital spending and debt obligations continue growing. No numerical threshold is imposed without a company-specific baseline.

The next work should answer the [prioritized investment questions](../../../investing/research-queue.md): what we already own through funds, what Alphabet earns after investment gains, and how to build credible portfolio projections. Operator financing remains a high-priority follow-up. Further general AGI summaries have less decision value than those tasks.
