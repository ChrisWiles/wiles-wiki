## The clearest investment evidence is now in company cash flows

Vertiv's October 28 release reports more than 100% organic order growth and raises guidance. GE Vernova raises its sales and cash outlook. These are direct signs that customers are committing to power and cooling infrastructure, although orders can still be cancelled or delivered at disappointing margins. [Vertiv results](https://investors.vertiv.com/news/news-details/2026/Vertiv-Reports-Strong-Third-Quarter-2026-with-Net-Sales-Growth-of-61-Organic-Orders-Growth-over-100-and-Diluted-EPS-Growth-of-91-Adjusted-Diluted-EPS-Growth-of-78-Raises-Full-Year-2026-Guidance-Across-All-Key-Metrics/default.aspx), [GE Vernova results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm).

Demand is also visible at the platforms: Alphabet's Cloud revenue grew 103%, Microsoft's Azure revenue 48%, and Amazon's AWS revenue about 39%. Those measures cover different products and accounting periods; Alphabet also sells TPU systems, so its Cloud growth is not a pure recurring-inference measure. [Alphabet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm), [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2027-Q1/press-release-webcast), [Amazon](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/default.aspx).

The uncomfortable part is the cash cost. Alphabet's quarterly operating cash minus equipment purchases was negative $9.065B; Amazon's reported trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was negative $42.357B. Microsoft generated $18.633B after cash purchases of property/equipment in its quarter, before a full lease and replacement-investment analysis. These are different periods and definitions, not a ranking of comparable owner cash. Fast revenue growth can coexist with heavy funding needs. That makes our existing wait on additional Alphabet and operator exposure more useful than a blanket buy-the-buildout conclusion.

## More chips sold is not the same as more profitable workloads

Epoch's October 29 study estimates that computing capacity in chips sold each quarter grew about 2.5 times per year from 2024, a roughly nine-month doubling. The fit uses ten quarters through Q2 2026 and six major designers. It counts chip sales, often months before installation, and its confidence interval excludes uncertainty in the underlying sales estimates. The authors do not find statistically significant slowing within the post-2024 sample. [Original study and methods](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-computing-capacity-additions-doubling).

This supports a large continuing buildout. It does not establish utilization, paying customer retention, end-user profitability or an acceleration in the latest quarter. Track those separately. The relationship we care about remains:

**Physical compute demand = useful workloads × compute required per workload.**

Better models and chips can reduce the second term. Whether usage expands enough to outweigh that improvement is still unresolved. Electricity demand adds energy efficiency, utilization and cooling. The [IEA's executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) continues to describe physical and financing constraints, but it is a conditional forecast, not a new regional grid or HBM inventory survey.

## An important check on our own investment process

Anthropic's October 27 experiment asked 50 professional analysts to use Claude Code for a causal-analysis task. Hidden, randomized instructions made each agent favor one of two defensible approaches. Analysts followed the agent's preference on 86% of the four analytical choices. Deliberately strengthened preferences, one fictional task and a selected sample limit how broadly that percentage applies. This does not measure AGI or prove that ordinary advice always has the same effect. [Study design, results and limits](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls).

For us, the implication is practical: a confident explanation is not independent evidence. Keep original decision reasoning, alternatives and disconfirming facts visible. A second AI agreeing with the first is not an independent accounting check. This week's reports therefore retain the cash negatives alongside the strongest demand evidence, and leave unsupported model parameters unchanged.

## What could weaken the thesis

Watch for spending that outruns profitable adoption, cancelled or delayed orders, price competition that eats supplier margins, and equipment replacement that consumes operator cash. A major efficiency gain could expand useful intelligence while reducing demand for a specific hardware category. Deployment reliability, approvals, grid connections and financing can also delay adoption after a capability breakthrough. None requires abandoning AI progress as a premise; each can change which shares earn attractive returns.
