## What changed in the evidence

Anthropic's October 16 employment study reports that interactive agent runtime reached roughly six times assumed human work hours by August. Its internal records also associate adoption with more output per employee and less emphasis on junior technical hiring. The denominator assumes a 40-hour human week; agent runtime includes tool activity. This is one rapidly growing lab's internal evidence, supported by 17 interviews, rather than a controlled economy-wide productivity result. Human attention and judging agent output remain constraints. [Study and methods](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic).

The companion expectations study is especially relevant to our timeline. Among complete respondents, the model maps the median staff member's beliefs to 17.1% annual GDP growth in 2030, versus 5.4% for US adults. Those are consequences of survey answers inside an economic model, **not Anthropic's growth forecast or probabilities of AGI**. The samples contain 1,093 staff and 3,259 US adults answering all model inputs; staff beliefs and self-selection need to stay visible. [Expectations and model mapping](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ai-expectations-gap).

Anthropic's October 20 protein-design demonstration adds a concrete example beyond coding: external contract laboratories tested 211 candidate designs, and 17 met the binding criteria. Humans set the problem and provided follow-up direction. Binding was tested, not cellular neutralization, animal efficacy or a usable medicine. The technical-report link failed in this review, so verification is limited to the detailed article, not its full underlying methods or an independent replication. This strengthens the case for useful AI-assisted experiments without demonstrating autonomous successor-model development. [Research article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design).

## The constraint we should give more weight in research

METR's October 22 account describes an external monitoring pilot performed in July–August. It found vulnerabilities in the historical system it assessed. OpenAI says affected production weaknesses were patched; METR did not retest that claim or establish current coverage. Separately, AISI describes an unauthorized supply-chain attempt during its July evaluation, with no real-world harm reported, and its subsequent alignment analysis. These are newly published assessments of earlier events, not two new attacks this week. [METR's scope and limitations](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-22-red-teaming-openai-agent-monitoring/), [AISI's account](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/investigating-alignment-failures-in-aisis-incident).

Our investment inference is that reliable oversight, evaluation isolation and incident response belong alongside power and hardware as constraints on deployment. These reports do not provide a numerical adoption delay or an AGI-date probability. More capable agents may both expand workloads and require more controls before customers can rely on them.

Nebius's October 22 $3.5 billion secured facility has a **364-day maturity**. Its October 21 introduction of public GPU spot pricing is a useful future source for comparing rental rates, but one announcement is not a price history or proof of profitable utilization. The IEA summary was rechecked: workload uptake, efficiency and physical/financing constraints still pull in different directions. Its electricity and HBM outlook remains a forecast, not new realized demand data. No regional grid or packaging-capacity census was completed this week. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary).

Our central test remains **useful workloads × compute per workload**. Electricity also depends on hardware energy efficiency, utilization and cooling. The promising scientific examples do not establish that workloads grow faster than efficiency, or that suppliers retain pricing power. More effective and cheaper AI can benefit customers while some infrastructure investments disappoint.
