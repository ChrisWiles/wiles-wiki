Trip planning · Austin · Autumn 2026

Five fall trips,
one week each

Three October foliage weeks in the East, two September park weeks out West — all from Austin, all built the same way: hike hard for the first half, downshift for the second. Every one of these places is ordinary two weeks off-peak and extraordinary inside its window, so the calendar comes first and the destination follows.

A

Vermont + White Mountains

Fri 2 → Fri 9 Oct

Two hard trail days in the New Hampshire notches while they peak, then four nights in Vermont as the color arrives there — villages, cider, a gondola instead of a climb. Nothing needs a reservation except the beds.

Flights
AUS ⇄ BOS nonstop
Delta / JetBlue / Southwest, 4h05
Bases
Franconia 3n
Stowe 4n
Driving
~450 mi
Estimate, two
$4,250–6,900
  1. Fri · Day 1

    Land, drive the notch

    Boston 12:28, north on I-93. Sugar Hill at golden hour for the first big view — a five-minute walk, not a hike. Dinner in Littleton.

  2. Sat · Day 2

    Franconia Ridge LoopHard

    8.6 mi, 3,900 ft, 6–8 hours. A mile and a half above treeline looking down on the color. Be in the Lafayette lot by 06:30 — it fills by seven.

  3. Sun · Day 3

    Kancamagus + Mount WillardHard

    The classic foliage highway east, then 3.2 mi to a cliff edge above Crawford Notch. Mount Washington Auto Road if the summits are clear.

  4. Mon · Day 4

    Cross into Vermont slowly

    Route 302 to St. Johnsbury, then Peacham — the most photographed village in the state. Stowe Pinnacle at day's end to stretch out the drive.

  5. Tue · Day 5

    Camel's Hump or MansfieldHard

    Mansfield via Sunset Ridge, 6.6 mi and 2,550 ft to the state high point — or Camel's Hump, shorter and steeper with no ski infrastructure on top.

  6. Wed · Day 6

    Smugglers' Notch, then stopEasy

    A one-lane squeeze between boulders, open only in the warm months. Afternoon spa at Topnotch or Spruce Peak. This is where the trip changes gear.

  7. Thu · Day 7

    Mad River ValleyEasy

    Route 100 south through Waitsfield and Warren, the covered bridge, Moss Glen Falls thirty seconds from the car. Last dinner at Hen of the Wood.

  8. Fri · Day 8

    Home via Woodstock

    Stowe to Logan is 3h30, or 4h30 through Woodstock and Quechee Gorge — worth the hour on an evening flight.

Why this week
  • White Mountains peak ~28 Sep – 8 Oct
  • Northern Vermont peaks first week of October; Essex County ~4 Oct
  • Statewide VT average ~9 Oct
  • Home Fri 9 Oct — ahead of Columbus Day weekend, the worst traffic and rates of the year
Sleep
  • Sugar Hill Inn — best in the notch, books out
  • Mountain Club on Loon — pragmatic, pool
  • Field Guide, Stowe — design-forward, good value
  • Lodge at Spruce Peak — the spa finish
Watch the drought. A warm, dry New England summer can mute color and drop leaves early. Re-check the foliage maps in mid-September; if the north is running fast, move the whole week four days earlier.
B

Maine coast + Acadia

Tue 13 → Tue 20 Oct

Granite domes falling straight into the Atlantic. The hiking here is short and vertical — iron rungs bolted into cliffs rather than long ridge walks — and the back half unwinds south through Camden into Portland.

Flights
AUS ⇄ BOS nonstop
no AUS→PWM nonstop exists
Bases
Portland 1n · Bar Harbor 4n
Camden 1n · Portland 1n
Driving
~600 mi
Estimate, two
$4,300–7,100
  1. Tue · Day 1

    Boston to Portland

    Two hours north, straight into the Old Port. Eventide for the brown-butter lobster roll, or Fore Street if you want the serious version.

  2. Wed · Day 2

    Up to Mount Desert Island

    Three hours, then orient: Ocean Path past Thunder Hole, Great Head for the first scramble, sunset at Bass Harbor Head Light.

  3. Thu · Day 3

    Cadillac sunrise, then the rungsHard

    From October, Cadillac is the first place in the country to see sunrise. Then the Beehive (1.5 mi of exposed iron rungs) and Precipice if it's open.

  4. Fri · Day 4

    Penobscot & SargentHard

    6 mi and 1,500 ft off Jordan Pond over the island's second summit — far quieter than Cadillac. Popovers after, or 11 mi of carriage road by bike.

  5. Sat · Day 5

    The quiet sideHard

    Beech Mountain and the western half, which sees a fraction of the traffic. Lobster off a dock in Bass Harbor rather than off a menu.

  6. Sun · Day 6

    South to CamdenEasy

    Mount Battie by car or on foot for the view over Penobscot Bay, then the Farnsworth in Rockland for the Wyeths.

  7. Mon · Day 7

    The long slow coastEasy

    Rockland's mile-long granite breakwater, Wiscasset, Freeport. Back to Portland for the final dinner.

  8. Tue · Day 8

    Home

    Portland Head Light and The Holy Donut, then two hours to Logan.

Why this week
  • Coastal Maine peaks 10–20 Oct — later than Vermont, and roughly two weeks later than it did in 1950
  • Arriving Tue 13 Oct lands the day the Columbus Day crowds leave, straight into peak
Reserve
  • Cadillac Summit Road, $6/vehicle — required through 25 Oct, Recreation.gov only. The 90-day release is gone; 70% drops at 10:00 EST two days prior and sells out in minutes.
  • Bar Harbor lodging — confirm it's open that late
October 15 is a cliff on Mount Desert Island. Assume the free Island Explorer shuttle has stopped, and verify every inn, restaurant, and boat individually. You'll be driving and parking yourself — fine in late October, when the lots are no longer full.

Asheville + Blue Ridge Parkway

Wed 14 → Wed 21 Oct

The only option where hike-hard-then-relax maps onto geography instead of willpower. Color descends about 1,000 vertical feet a week, so you start high in the balds at their peak and end in Asheville as color arrives in town.

Flights
AUS → CLT nonstop + 2h05
AVL nonstop is ~2×/week
Bases
Waynesville 3n
Asheville 4n
Driving
~500 mi
Estimate, two
$3,750–6,350
  1. Wed · Day 1

    Straight past Asheville

    Charlotte, then I-40 west to Waynesville. On the Parkway before dark for sunset near Mount Pisgah.

  2. Thu · Day 2

    Black Balsam and the baldsHard

    The best day of the trip. Grassy open summits at 6,000 ft — you see the whole range of color instead of looking at it through trunks. Add Graveyard Fields and Devil's Courthouse.

  3. Fri · Day 3

    Looking Glass RockHard

    6.5 mi and 1,700 ft of switchbacks to a bare granite dome. Or drive to Max Patch for an Appalachian Trail bald with 1.5 mi of walking.

  4. Sat · Day 4

    Smokies, then into townHard

    Newfound Gap by 08:00, Kuwohi and Andrews Bald. Then east to Asheville for the second half.

  5. Sun · Day 5

    Biltmore and downtownEasy

    Half a day minimum — the house, then Olmsted's last major landscape, which is engineered for exactly this week. Cúrate for dinner, booked weeks out.

  6. Mon · Day 6

    Do very littleEasy

    Shoji Retreat's outdoor soaking tubs, apple orchards down in Hendersonville, and a brewery afternoon. Or the Grove Park terrace at sunset.

  7. Tue · Day 7

    One more Parkway runEasy

    Color has dropped a thousand feet since Thursday. Waterrock Knob at MP 451 is the best sunset on the whole road.

  8. Wed · Day 8

    Home

    Two hours back to Charlotte, or the short hop from AVL.

The elevation ladder
  • 6,000 ft+ — mid-to-late September
  • 5,000 ft — first week of October
  • 4–5,000 ft — second week
  • 3–4,000 ft — third week
  • Asheville, 2,100 ft — fourth week
Shift later?
  • 21–28 Oct puts Biltmore and downtown at their absolute best — but the balds will be bare sticks by then.
Parkway repairs are still uneven. South of Asheville (MP 382 to Cherokee) is fully open, and this whole itinerary lives there deliberately. North of MP 382 toward Mount Mitchell is an active construction corridor — dump trucks, a 35 mph limit, and sections not due to reopen until late 2026.
D

Yellowstone + Grand Teton

Sat 19 → Sat 26 Sep

Not a foliage trip — a wildlife and geology trip that happens to have gold aspens in it. Late September is the best week of the year in Yellowstone: the elk rut at full volume, visitor numbers down 40% after Labor Day, and everything still open.

Flights
AUS ⇄ BZN nonstop
Delta, Saturdays only
Bases
W. Yellowstone 1n · Jackson 3n
Canyon 1n · Paradise Valley 2n
Driving
~900 mi
Estimate, two
$4,950–7,900
  1. Sat · Day 1

    The Gallatin Canyon

    Two hours south from Bozeman along a river gorge the whole way. Early night — you're at 6,600 ft now and will feel it.

  2. Sun · Day 2

    Geyser basins, cold morning

    Every thermal feature throws steam in September air. Grand Geyser beats Old Faithful if you can time it. Fairy Falls trail to the Grand Prismatic overlook — the only way to actually see its colors.

  3. Mon · Day 3

    Cascade CanyonHard

    Jenny Lake boat to Inspiration Point, then up a glacial U between 12,000-ft walls with moose in the willows. Verify the boat is still running.

  4. Tue · Day 4

    Delta LakeHard

    8 mi, 2,300 ft, ending in an unmaintained boulder scramble to a lake the color of antifreeze directly under the Grand. Dawn at Oxbow Bend first.

  5. Wed · Day 5

    North through the parkHard

    Hayden Valley bison, the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, and Mount Washburn if there's another summit in you.

  6. Thu · Day 6

    Lamar Valley and Mammoth

    Pre-dawn for wolves — find the tripods, talk to those people. Then elk bugling on the lawns at Mammoth, which in rut week means keeping real distance.

  7. Fri · Day 7

    Chico Hot SpringsEasy

    Two open-air mineral pools at a 1900 lodge, mountains on every side. The dining room is the best food within fifty miles. Or float the Yellowstone for trout.

  8. Sat · Day 8

    Home

    Chico to Bozeman is 75 minutes, with Livingston on the way for a last coffee.

Why September
  • Elk rut peaks mid-Sep to early Oct — Mammoth, Madison Junction, Gibbon Meadows
  • Crowds drop 40% after Labor Day (Mon 7 Sep)
  • Highs 58–67 °F late month, lows below freezing
  • Roosevelt Lodge closed 7 Sep; other in-park lodges start closing late September
Altitude
  • Austin sits at 500 ft. Jackson is 6,200, trailheads 6,700, Delta Lake near 9,000.
  • Days 1–2 are deliberately easy so the big hikes land on acclimatised legs.
The Saturday nonstop sets the dates. Delta's AUS→BZN service launched 13 June 2026 and runs Saturdays only, seasonally. Confirm it still operates on 19 and 26 September before booking anything else — otherwise connect through Salt Lake or Denver.
E

Glacier

Sat 12 → Sat 19 Sep

The best hiking of the five, without much argument. Going-to-the-Sun Road gets driven as a transfer rather than a day trip, so you cross the best road in America with your luggage in the back and never repeat it.

Flights
AUS ⇄ FCA nonstop, 3h55
Delta, Saturdays only
Bases
West Glacier 3n · Many Glacier 2n
Whitefish 2n
Airport to park
40 minutes
Estimate, two
$4,500–7,250
  1. Sat · Day 1

    Lake McDonald

    Easy first day at altitude: the cedar boardwalk out to Avalanche Lake, 4.5 mi to a bowl ringed with waterfalls. Sunset from the Apgar dock.

  2. Sun · Day 2

    Highline TrailHard

    The signature hike — the Continental Divide with a hundred-mile view and a hand cable bolted into the ledge. Add the Grinnell Glacier Overlook spur if legs allow.

  3. Mon · Day 3

    Two MedicineHard

    The park's quietest corner and its best aspen stands. Scenic Point, 7.4 mi and 2,300 ft, or the boat to Upper Two Medicine Lake.

  4. Tue · Day 4

    Over the Sun Road

    West to east: The Loop, Bird Woman Falls, the Weeping Wall, Logan Pass, Wild Goose Island. Walk Hidden Lake Overlook — mountain goats basically guaranteed.

  5. Wed · Day 5

    Grinnell GlacierHard

    Three lakes, each a more impossible turquoise than the last, up to a glacier and an iceberg tarn. 10.6 mi, or 7.6 with the two boats.

  6. Thu · Day 6

    Iceberg Lake, then west

    9.7 mi but surprisingly gentle for the payoff — or a slow breakfast on the Many Glacier deck instead. Whitefish by evening.

  7. Fri · Day 7

    Whitefish, deliberately idleEasy

    A real town rather than a gateway strip. The lake, the gondola if you want a summit without walking, a spa afternoon, larch on the Hungry Horse loop.

  8. Sat · Day 8

    Home

    Thirty minutes to the airport. The easiest departure of all five options.

What's new in 2026
  • No vehicle reservations at all — the system was dropped this year
  • Three-hour parking limit at Logan Pass, plus a new ticketed Logan Pass Shuttle for Highline hikers
  • Many Glacier construction is finished; none anticipated this season
The week trade-off
  • 12–19 Sep — everything open, shuttles running, low snow risk, color barely starting
  • 19–26 Sep — larch and aspen turning, emptiest trails, but lodges closing and snow possible on Logan Pass
  • If Glacier is the hiking trip and Vermont is the fall trip, take the earlier week and don't compromise the hiking.
Bears. The Many Glacier valley has high grizzly activity, and September is hyperphagia season — they're feeding hard before denning. Spray on the belt, not in the pack, and check trail status at the ranger station each morning.

The call

If you pick one: Vermont, 2–9 October

Most reliable color, cleanest flights, the best hard-then-easy structure — Franconia Ridge on the Saturday, a Stowe spa by the Wednesday — and almost nothing to reserve beyond beds. It also gets you home before Columbus Day weekend.

If you pick two: add Glacier, 12–19 September

They don't compete. September Montana and October New England are different trips in different seasons, and the new Saturday nonstop to Kalispell makes Glacier unusually easy to reach from Austin this year. Glacier is the better hiking; Vermont is the better fall.

Calendar reality

Asia is locked for 13–29 November. One September trip plus one October trip means roughly five weeks away between mid-September and the end of November — workable, with two clear weeks between each leg. Three trips stacks a mid-October return against a 13 November long-haul.

Book in this order

  1. Lodging. Peak-week inventory in Stowe, Bar Harbor, Many Glacier and Jackson is already thin in August; the best properties go 6–12 months out.
  2. Rental cars. Every one of these is car-dependent, and Kalispell and Bozeman supply is genuinely tight.
  3. Flights last — the most flexible piece and the least likely to sell out. Except the Montana Saturday nonstops, which need confirming first.
  4. America the Beautiful pass, $80 — pays for itself the moment you visit a second national park.
