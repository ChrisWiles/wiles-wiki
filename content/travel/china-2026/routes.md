Seven route variants for the **Fall 2026 trip envelope** (baseline: depart AUS Thu **8 Oct 2026**, return AUS Thu **22 Oct 2026** — 13 in-country nights; see [[routes#When to go — the dates are flexible|When to go]] for the recommended November shift). Every variant fits **inside the 240-hour TWOV window**. No L visa required for any route.

This page exists so the group can compare side-by-side and pick one. Each route is a complete itinerary with day-by-day stops, TWOV clock, transport, and trade-offs. Destination pages have the on-the-ground detail.

## TWOV primer (read this first)

**240-hour transit-without-visa (TWOV)** is the current Mainland China policy that lets US passport holders stay up to 240 hours (≈10 days) with no visa, as long as the trip starts in one country and ends in a *different* country/region. Hong Kong **counts** as a different region for this purpose, so a trip flowing AUS → mainland → HK → AUS qualifies.

Key rules:
- **Clock**: 240 hours starts at **00:00 of the day after entry**. So your arrival day is a freebie. Latest mainland exit = midnight of Day 11 from arrival.
- **24 eligible regions** include every place on this page: Shanghai, Zhejiang (Hangzhou), Anhui (Huangshan), Fujian (Xiamen), Guangdong (Guangzhou/Shenzhen), and **Hunan (Zhangjiajie — added in the Nov 2024 expansion)**.
- **Eligible entry/exit ports** include PVG (Shanghai Pudong), SHA (Hongqiao), SZX (Shenzhen Bao'an), Shekou Ferry Terminal, Luohu / Lo Wu / Lok Ma Chau / Shenzhen Bay land borders, and — as of **Nov 2025** — the **West Kowloon HSR station in Hong Kong**, plus the **HK–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge**. Zhangjiajie DYG is listed by major travel sources as a 240-hr port but I have not seen direct NIA confirmation — safest play is to use a verified port for entry/exit and have DYG only as an internal hop.
- **Documents at port**: passport with ≥3 months validity, onward ticket to a third country/region within 240 hrs, completed arrival card.
- HK and Macau airports are also "third regions" — meaning if you exit mainland to HK, then fly from HK to AUS, the chain is compliant.

**Sources**: [NIA TWOV page](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c183412/content.html), [gov.cn Nov 2025 expansion](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202511/04/content_WS69094ae0c6d00ca5f9a07472.html), [MTR West Kowloon TWOV notice](https://www.highspeed.mtr.com.hk/en/latest-news/240-hr-visa-freetransit-policy.html).

### Shenzhen 5-day VOA — not useful for these routes

The Shenzhen Special Economic Zone 5-day port visa is **available to US passport holders** in 2026 at the **Luohu, Huanggang, and Shekou** crossings (~CNY 956 cash, ~30–60 min processing). **But it permits travel inside Shenzhen municipality only** — it cannot be combined with travel to Shanghai, Hangzhou, Huangshan, etc. So it's useful for an HK-and-Shenzhen-only trip, not for any of the routes here. **All five routes rely purely on TWOV.**

### New for 2026: the overnight HSR sleeper (Routes B–E upgrade)

The **HK West Kowloon → Shanghai Hongqiao high-speed sleeper (G899/G900)** went **daily in January 2026**. Departs HK ~19:49, arrives Hongqiao 06:45. For any route that starts the mainland leg with the HK → PVG flight, the sleeper is strictly better:

- **Soft sleeper ~CNY 900 (~$132/person)** vs ~$200 flight — and the train *is* that night's hotel (~$150–250 saved again)
- Arrives **city-center Hongqiao** on the metro, not PVG 45 min out
- You keep the **full final HK day** instead of losing the morning to HKG
- **Same TWOV math**: board the evening of Oct 11 → immigration at West Kowloon that evening → clock still starts 00:00 Oct 12, identical to flying that afternoon
- Book via MTR high-speed channels / 12306 when the 15-day window opens; the deluxe cabin (bunk + sofa) is the group-comfort pick

Sources: [seat61](https://www.seat61.com/HongKong.htm), [MTR fares](https://www.highspeed.mtr.com.hk/en/ticket/fare.html).

## When to go — the dates are flexible

The Oct 8–22 baseline above works, but the group is flexible across Oct–Nov, and **two collisions argue for a November shift**:

1. **Canton Fair (Routes A + F)** — autumn 2026 phases run **Oct 15–19, Oct 23–27, Oct 31–Nov 4**. Guangzhou hotels run 2–3× normal and sell out during phases; the baseline Route A Guangzhou stay (Oct 13–16) hits Phase 1 arrival days.
2. **HK trade-fair season** — mid-Oct through early Nov is HK's high hotel corridor (Electronics, Optical, Wine fairs). Late Nov is noticeably cheaper.

Meanwhile November *improves* the trip: HK's best month (23°C/18°C, typhoon risk ≈ zero), Shanghai's plane trees turn golden, Huangshan hits peak foliage + best sea-of-clouds odds (late Oct–mid Nov), and Zhangjiajie's most-recommended window is the first half of November. Transpacific fares stay October-level until Thanksgiving week (Nov 26).

**Recommended windows** (all Thursdays, keeping the 13-night envelope):

| If we pick | Fly | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Route A / G / F | **Thu Nov 5 → Thu Nov 19** | Guangzhou fully post-fair (A/F); Shanghai golden-foliage prime (A/G); home a week before Thanksgiving |
| Route B / C / E | **Thu Oct 29 → Thu Nov 12** | Huangshan / Zhangjiajie land Nov 5–8: peak foliage, prime sea-of-clouds |

**Weekday tune-up**: shifting the departure to a **Tuesday** (e.g., Tue Nov 3 → Tue Nov 17) puts the Wuzhen guesthouse nights on **Wed–Thu instead of Fri–Sat** — cheaper, emptier canals, which is the whole point of sleeping inside Xizha. Weekday transpacific departures also usually price lower.

**Pack note**: the Nov windows need one extra warm layer — Huangshan summit nights near 0°C, Shanghai evenings ~10°C. See [[packing]].

## My take

If we want the **locked, easy, food-and-scenery trip**: **Route A** (current plan). Wuzhen is the trip-maker — nothing else in this lineup has that same slow canal-town night, and exiting direct via PVG saves a day.

If we want the **foodie-first version of Route A**: **Route G**. Same skeleton, but the food-city slot goes to Chongqing — nine-grid hotpot, xiaomian, and the Hongya Cave nightscape. For a group of friends who eat, this is A's strongest challenger.

If we want **karst drama with the least risk**: **Route F**. Zero flights, ~4 days of TWOV buffer, six HK nights, and the Li River — it tops the matrix on paper. The catch: no Shanghai, no Wuzhen, and Yangshuo's food is solid-but-simpler (beer fish, Guilin mifen) rather than a destination cuisine.

If we want **mountains and lake without too many trains**: **Route C**. Drops Xiamen (the obvious cuttable stop in Route B) and gives Hangzhou + Huangshan their real time. The most "Chinese landscape painting" version of this trip.

If we want **Kaylee's Avatar peaks**: **Route E**. The internal flights are a real cost but Zhangjiajie genuinely doesn't look like anywhere else. Don't try to add it onto another route. (Route F gets a cousin of that landscape by rail — worth comparing before committing to E's flights.)

I'd avoid **Route B** — 11 hours of train time across 5 segments inside a TWOV-tight window means one slipped train, missed connection, or weather hold turns into a real problem. Either pick Route C (which is most of Route B) or commit to Route E for the Zhangjiajie payoff. **Route D** is the safe answer if jet lag is a concern and we just want two big cities; it's the easiest one to actually execute.
