**Recommended dates: Fri 2 Oct → Fri 9 Oct 2026 (7 nights).** Fly AUS ⇄ BOS nonstop, one rental car, two bases.

This is the highest-confidence color of the five options and the best hike-hard-then-relax structure: the White Mountains give you two hard trail days up front, then Vermont downshifts into villages, cider, and a spa.

## Why these dates

| Area | 2026 forecast peak | Our days there |
|---|---|---|
| White Mountains, NH (notches, 2,000–4,000 ft) | ~28 Sep – 8 Oct | Oct 2–5 |
| Northeast Kingdom / Essex County, VT | ~4 Oct | drive-through Oct 5 |
| Lamoille County (Stowe, Mansfield) | first week of Oct | Oct 5–9 |
| Statewide Vermont average | ~9 Oct | Oct 5–9 |
| Addison County / Champlain Valley | ~12 Oct | — |

The forecast band is roughly ±10 days, so treat this as "the statistically best single week," not a guarantee. Departing Fri Oct 9 also gets you out **before Columbus Day weekend (Oct 10–12)**, which is the single worst weekend of the year for Vermont traffic and lodging rates.

**Conditions caveat for 2026:** New England has been in a warm, dry summer, and August stayed dry around Boston. Drought can mute color and drop leaves early. Re-check the [Vermont foliage map](https://www.explorefall.com/states/vermont.html) in mid-September; if the north is running early, shift the whole week ~4 days earlier.

## Flights

- **AUS → BOS nonstop** on Delta, JetBlue, and Southwest — about 100 nonstops a week, ~4h05 block time. Earliest departure 07:20 arriving 12:28; latest 19:10 arriving 23:59.
- Take the **morning departure out** (lands 12:28, you're in Franconia by 15:00 with daylight to spare) and an **evening flight home** so the last day is usable.
- Estimated fare: **$300–450 round trip per person** booked now. Not verified — check actual dates.

## Car

Full-size or small SUV out of BOS. Foliage week is peak demand in New England, so book now. Estimate **$450–700 for the week**. You do not need AWD in early October, but you do want something that handles a dirt road without drama.

## The route

```
BOS ──2h15──▶ Franconia / Lincoln, NH (3 nights)
      ──2h30──▶ Stowe / Waterbury, VT (4 nights)
      ──3h30──▶ BOS
```

## Day by day

### Day 1 — Fri Oct 2 · Arrive, settle into the notch
Land Boston midday, pick up the car, drive I-93 north (2h15). Late afternoon: **Sugar Hill / Sunset Hill Road** for the first big view — open meadows, Franconia Ridge as backdrop, and it's a five-minute walk, not a hike. Dinner at **Woodstock Inn Station & Brewery** in North Woodstock or **Schilling Beer Co.** in Littleton (riverside, wood-fired pizza). Early night — tomorrow starts before dawn.

### Day 2 — Sat Oct 3 · Franconia Ridge Loop (the big one)
**8.6 mi, ~3,900 ft gain, 6–8 hours.** Falling Waters Trail → Little Haystack → Mt. Lincoln → Mt. Lafayette → Greenleaf Trail → Old Bridle Path. The above-treeline mile and a half along the ridge is the best single stretch of trail in the Northeast, and in early October you're looking down on the color rather than through it.

- **Parking is the hard part.** Lafayette Place lot fills by 07:00 on an October Saturday. Be in the lot by 06:30 or use the Skookumchuck overflow.
- Above treeline it can be 35°F with 40 mph wind while the valley is 60°F and calm. Hard shell, hat, gloves, headlamp, real food. Check the [Mount Washington Observatory higher-summits forecast](https://mountwashington.org/weather/higher-summit-forecast/) the night before.
- **Bad-weather swap:** Artists Bluff (1.5 mi), Lonesome Lake (3.2 mi), and Flume Gorge — three short ones that still deliver, plus the Cannon Mountain tram.

Recovery dinner in Lincoln.

### Day 3 — Sun Oct 4 · Kancamagus + a shorter summit
Drive the **Kancamagus Highway** east (34 mi, no services, the classic foliage road). Stops: Sabbaday Falls (0.6 mi), Rocky Gorge, Sugar Hill Overlook, Hancock Overlook.

Pick one hike:
- **Mount Willard** — 3.2 mi, 900 ft, the best payoff-per-effort in the Whites. You stand on a cliff edge looking straight down Crawford Notch.
- **Welch–Dickey Loop** — 4.4 mi, 1,800 ft, open granite ledges the whole way. Harder, and better if legs are cooperating.

Afternoon option if the summits are clear: **Mount Washington Auto Road** (drive it yourself or take the guided van) or the **Cog Railway**. Both are weather-dependent and both are worth it on a clear day.

### Day 4 — Mon Oct 5 · Cross into Vermont the slow way
Route 302 west → **St. Johnsbury** (Fairbanks Museum, Athenaeum) → **Peacham**, which is the most photographed village in Vermont for good reason → Cabot → Route 2 to Waterbury.

Check into the Stowe-area base. Late afternoon: **Stowe Pinnacle** (2.8 mi, 1,500 ft) — a short, steep leg-stretcher with a view straight across at Mount Mansfield. Dinner in Stowe village.

### Day 5 — Tue Oct 6 · Camel's Hump or Mansfield
The second hard day. Choose:
- **Mount Mansfield via Sunset Ridge** (6.6 mi, 2,550 ft) — Vermont's high point, long open ridge, views to the Adirondacks and Whites.
- **Camel's Hump via Burrows Trail** (4.8 mi, 2,000 ft) — shorter, steeper, the most recognizable summit in the state and no ski infrastructure on top.

Afternoon: the **Stowe Recreation Path** to loosen up, then **The Alchemist** for Heady Topper (retail only, check hours) or **Stowe Cider**.

### Day 6 — Wed Oct 7 · Downshift day
No alarm. **Smugglers' Notch (Route 108)** north — a genuinely dramatic one-lane squeeze between boulders, open in October, closed all winter. Jeffersonville on the far side for lunch.

Afternoon is the relax half of the trip: book a **spa afternoon at Topnotch or the Lodge at Spruce Peak**, or ride the **Mansfield gondola** up instead of walking. Evening: **Hill Farmstead** in Greensboro Bend if you want the pilgrimage (1h drive, limited hours — verify before committing), otherwise Fiddlehead or von Trapp Brewing closer in.

### Day 7 — Thu Oct 8 · Mad River Valley
Route 100 south through **Waitsfield and Warren** — the covered bridge, Warren Store, **Moss Glen Falls** in Granville (roadside, 30 seconds from the car), and the Mad River Valley farms. This is the postcard drive of the trip. Sugarbush for lunch with a view.

Last dinner: a proper one. **Hen of the Wood** in Waterbury, **Michael's on the Hill**, or **Doc Ponds** if you want it casual.

### Day 8 — Fri Oct 9 · Home the scenic way
Stowe → Boston is 3h30 direct, or 4h30 with a stop in **Woodstock, VT** and **Quechee Gorge** — worth the extra hour if you're on an evening flight. Fuel up before Logan, gas near the airport is highway robbery.

## Lodging

**Franconia / Lincoln (3 nights)** — [Sugar Hill Inn](https://www.sugarhillinn.com/) is the best in the area and books out; [Franconia Inn](https://www.franconiainn.com/) is more relaxed with horses and a view; [Mountain Club on Loon](https://www.mtnclub.com/) is the pragmatic option with a pool and ski-lodge bones.

**Stowe / Waterbury (4 nights)** — [Field Guide](https://www.fieldguidestowe.com/) (design-y, good value), [Green Mountain Inn](https://www.greenmountaininn.com/) (in the village, walk to dinner), [Topnotch](https://www.topnotchresort.com/) or [Lodge at Spruce Peak](https://www.sprucepeak.com/) for the splurge-and-spa finish.

Budget **$250–500/night** for peak foliage week. Rates and availability are unverified — the good inns for Oct 2–9 are already thin in August, so this is the piece to lock first.

## Book now, in this order

1. **Stowe-area lodging for Oct 5–9** — the binding constraint.
2. Franconia/Lincoln lodging for Oct 2–5.
3. Rental car (free cancellation, rebook if it drops).
4. Flights.
5. Nothing else needs a reservation, which is part of why this option is easy.

## Risks

- **Drought muting the color** — the real 2026 wildcard. Watch the September foliage maps.
- **Franconia Ridge weather** — a windy, socked-in day makes the marquee hike pointless and mildly dangerous. Keep Day 2 and Day 3 swappable.
- **Columbus Day creep** — Thu/Fri Oct 8–9 will already be filling with holiday-weekend arrivals.
