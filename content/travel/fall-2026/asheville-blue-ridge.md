**Recommended dates: Wed 14 Oct → Wed 21 Oct 2026 (7 nights).** Fly AUS → CLT or AVL, one rental car, two bases.

The cheapest, warmest, and least logistically fussy of the three eastern options — and the only one where the *hike hard, then relax* split maps onto geography instead of willpower. Color descends the mountains at roughly 1,000 vertical feet per week, so you start high in the balds while they're peaking and end in Asheville as color arrives in town.

## Why these dates

| Elevation | Peak window | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 6,000+ ft | mid–late September | Mt. Mitchell, Richland Balsam |
| 5,000–6,000 ft | first week of October | Black Balsam, Mt. Pisgah, Devil's Courthouse |
| 4,000–5,000 ft | second week of October | Max Patch, Roan Mountain, the Smokies |
| 3,000–4,000 ft | third week of October | most Parkway overlooks, Brevard, Pisgah Forest |
| 2,000–3,000 ft | fourth week of October | **Asheville, Biltmore**, Sylva, Saluda |
| 1,000–2,000 ft | first week of November | Chimney Rock, Lake Lure, the foothills |

Oct 14–21 puts the high hikes at the tail of their peak, the mid-elevation Parkway squarely in it, and Asheville itself just starting to turn as you move into town. It also dodges Columbus Day weekend, when Parkway overlook parking becomes a contact sport.

**Want maximum in-town color instead?** Shift to **Oct 21–28** — Biltmore and downtown at their absolute best, but the 5,000-ft balds will be bare.

## Parkway status — read this before committing

Hurricane Helene damage is still being repaired, and the picture is uneven:

- **South of Asheville is fully open** — 85+ miles from milepost 382 down to the southern terminus near Cherokee. This is where nearly everything in this itinerary lives (Mt. Pisgah MP 408, Graveyard Fields MP 418, Black Balsam MP 420, Devil's Courthouse MP 422, Waterrock Knob MP 451).
- **North of Asheville is an active construction corridor.** Between MP 382.5 and Mount Mitchell (MP 355.3), expect heavy dump-truck traffic, a **35 mph limit**, and delays. Sections between Mount Mitchell and Little Switzerland are slated to reopen **late 2026** — possibly after your trip.
- Craggy Gardens (MP 364) sits inside that corridor. Reachable, but not the relaxed drive it used to be.

Check [NPS road status](https://www.nps.gov/blri/planyourvisit/roadclosures.htm) the week before you go — this changes.

## Flights

- **AUS → AVL nonstop is thin** — Allegiant runs about 2 flights a week, with limited Delta service. If the schedule happens to line up with your dates, take it: AVL is 20 minutes from downtown Asheville.
- **Reliable alternative: AUS → CLT nonstop** (American), then a **2h05 drive** on I-40. More flexible, usually cheaper, and you need the car anyway.
- **AUS → ATL** (Delta) is also nonstop, but it's a 3h30 drive from Atlanta — only worth it on a big fare gap.
- **GSP (Greenville/Spartanburg)** is the closest alternative at 1h20 from Asheville, but requires a connection from Austin.
- Estimated fare: **$250–450 RT per person**.

## The route

```
CLT ──2h05──▶ Waynesville / Brevard (3 nights)   [high country, hike hard]
      ──45m──▶ Asheville (4 nights)              [town, food, spa]
      ──2h05──▶ CLT
```

## Day by day

### Day 1 — Wed Oct 14 · Land and get high
Fly into CLT, drive I-40 west. Skip Asheville for now and continue to **Waynesville** or **Brevard** (another 45 min). Get on the Parkway before dark for **sunset at Cold Mountain Overlook or the Mt. Pisgah area** — MP 408–412, all in the open southern section. Dinner in Waynesville.

### Day 2 — Thu Oct 15 · The balds (the best day of the trip)
**Black Balsam Knob → Tennent Mountain → Ivestor Gap** on the Art Loeb Trail, MP 420. Four to seven miles depending on how far you push, at 6,000 ft, and — crucially — **above the treeline**. These are grassy open balds, so you get uninterrupted 360° views over an entire mountain range of color rather than looking at it through trunks. There is nothing like it in the Southeast.

Stack on the way back:
- **Graveyard Fields** (MP 418, 3.2 mi loop, two waterfalls, blueberry meadows that go crimson)
- **Devil's Courthouse** (MP 422, 0.8 mi, steep, huge payoff)

Parking at Black Balsam fills by mid-morning on clear days. Go early.

### Day 3 — Fri Oct 16 · Looking Glass or Max Patch
Pick your second hard day:
- **Looking Glass Rock** — 6.5 mi, 1,700 ft, Pisgah National Forest. Switchbacks up to a bare granite dome with the whole valley below.
- **Max Patch** — a grassy Appalachian Trail bald, only 1.5 mi of walking but 1h15 of driving from Waynesville. Day-use rules apply (no camping, restricted hours) — check current regulations.
- **Shining Rock Wilderness** if you want something long and unmarked.

Afternoon: **Looking Glass Falls** (roadside), **Sliding Rock**, then Brevard for beer at **Oskar Blues** or **Ecusta**.

### Day 4 — Sat Oct 17 · Smokies day, then move to town
**Great Smoky Mountains** via Newfound Gap Road, ~1h15 from Waynesville. **Kuwohi** (formerly Clingmans Dome, renamed in 2024) is the park's high point at 6,643 ft; the paved half-mile to the observation tower is steep but short. **Andrews Bald** (3.6 mi) is the better walk. **Charlies Bunion** (8 mi from Newfound Gap) if you want a real day.

Saturday in the Smokies is busy — be at Newfound Gap by 08:00 or accept the traffic. Afterward, drive into **Asheville** for the second half. Nights 4–7.

### Day 5 — Sun Oct 18 · Biltmore and downtown
**Biltmore Estate** — timed-entry tickets, book ahead. Give it a half day minimum: the house, then the gardens and the winery. The estate grounds are Frederick Law Olmsted's last major project, and mid-to-late October is when they look the way he intended.

Afternoon: **downtown Asheville** on foot — the River Arts District, Grove Arcade, Malaprop's. Dinner at **Cúrate** (Spanish tapas, book weeks ahead), **Chai Pani**, or **Rhubarb**.

### Day 6 — Mon Oct 19 · Slow day
This is the relax half. Options, pick two:
- **Shoji Retreat** — outdoor Japanese soaking tubs on a wooded hillside, private hour-long bookings. The single best recovery move in Asheville.
- **Hot Springs, NC** — mineral tubs on the French Broad, 1h north
- **Hendersonville / Flat Rock apple orchards** — October is apple season here, and it's the real thing
- **Brewery afternoon** — Burial, Wicked Weed Funkatorium (sours), Green Man, or Sierra Nevada's Mills River campus, which is absurdly nice
- **Grove Park Inn** for the subterranean spa and a drink on the terrace at sunset

### Day 7 — Tue Oct 20 · Last Parkway run
Head back onto the southern Parkway now that color has dropped a thousand feet: **Waterrock Knob** (MP 451, 1.2 mi steep, best sunset on the whole road) or the **Craggy Gardens** stretch north if the construction traffic is tolerable. **Chimney Rock State Park** is a possibility — verify current access, the village below it was hit hard by Helene and recovery is ongoing.

Final dinner in Asheville.

### Day 8 — Wed Oct 21 · Home
Drive to CLT (2h05, leave 3h30 before departure) or hop the short flight from AVL.

## Lodging

**Waynesville / Brevard (3 nights)** — [The Swag](https://theswag.com/) is the destination splurge, a 5,000-ft mountaintop inn on the Smokies boundary; [The Andon-Reid Inn](https://www.andonreidinn.com/) or [Sunset Motel](https://thesunsetmotel.com/) in Brevard for character at a fraction of the price.

**Asheville (4 nights)** — [The Restoration](https://therestorationhotel.com/), [Kimpton Hotel Arras](https://www.hotelarras.com/), or [Grand Bohemian](https://www.bohemianhotelasheville.com/) in Biltmore Village. Montford B&Bs if you want quiet and porches.

Estimate **$200–400/night** — noticeably cheaper than Stowe or Bar Harbor in peak week.

## Risks

- **Parkway construction north of Asheville** may still be limiting in October. Everything in this plan is deliberately south of MP 382 for that reason.
- **Elevation timing is unforgiving in one direction** — arrive a week late and the balds are bare sticks. If you want to hedge, Oct 14–21 is the widest window because you'll always have *something* at peak somewhere on the elevation ladder. That hedge is the real argument for this option.
- **The color is genuinely different** — oaks, hickories, and sourwood give russet, gold, and deep red over a longer, subtler season. Vermont's sugar maples are louder. Both are good; they're not the same picture.
