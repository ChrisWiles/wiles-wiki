**Recommended dates: Sat 19 Sep → Sat 26 Sep 2026 (7 nights).** Fly AUS ⇄ BZN, one rental car, four bases in a loop.

Not a foliage trip — a **wildlife and geology trip that happens to have gold aspens in it**. Late September is arguably the best week of the entire year in Yellowstone: the elk rut is at full volume, visitor numbers drop about 40% after Labor Day, cottonwoods and aspens turn, and everything is still open.

## Why these dates

- **Labor Day is Mon Sep 7, 2026.** Everything after that is quieter.
- **Elk rut peaks mid-September through early October** — bull elk bugling at Mammoth Hot Springs, Madison Junction, and Gibbon Meadows. This is the sound of the trip and you will not forget it.
- **Aspen and cottonwood gold** hits late September, best around Oxbow Bend and the Teton valley floor.
- **Weather:** September highs average 68°F dropping to 58–67°F late in the month, with overnight lows regularly below freezing. Expect one snow event; it melts by noon.
- **Closures:** Roosevelt Lodge Cabins closed Sep 7, 2026. Other in-park lodges start closing in late September, with most shut by mid-October — verify each property's exact date on the [Yellowstone Lodges closing dates page](https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/stay/plan/opening-and-closing-dates/).

## Flights — the constraint that sets the dates

**Delta launched AUS → BZN nonstop on June 13, 2026 — Saturdays only**, operated by SkyWest as Delta Connection, seasonal "through the fall." That single fact is why this is a **Saturday-to-Saturday** itinerary.

- **Verify the last operating Saturday before booking anything else.** If service has ended by Sep 19, connect through SLC (Delta) or DEN (United) — both easy, and Bozeman is a well-served airport with 27 nonstop destinations.
- Estimated fare: **$350–550 RT per person**.
- Rent a **full-size SUV or 4WD** — not for the roads, which are paved, but for clearance on gravel pullouts and warmth at 6:00 a.m. Estimate **$550–800 for the week**. Bozeman rental supply is tight; book early.

## The route

```
BZN ──2h00──▶ West Yellowstone (1 night)
      ──3h30──▶ Jackson / Teton Village (3 nights)   [hike hard]
      ──2h30──▶ Canyon or Lake (1 night)
      ──2h00──▶ Paradise Valley / Chico (2 nights)   [relax]
      ──1h15──▶ BZN
```

Counter-clockwise, so the hard hiking is front-loaded in the Tetons and the trip ends in a hot spring an hour from the airport.

## Day by day

### Day 1 — Sat Sep 19 · Land and drive the Gallatin
BZN, pick up the car, and take **US-191 south through Gallatin Canyon** — a river gorge the whole way, better than the interstate alternative. Two hours to **West Yellowstone**. Dinner and an early night; you're at 6,600 ft now and will feel it.

### Day 2 — Sun Sep 20 · Geyser basins, then south
Enter at West Yellowstone at first light. The geyser basins are best in cold morning air, when every thermal feature is throwing steam.

- **Upper Geyser Basin** — Old Faithful plus the 2-mile boardwalk loop past Castle, Grand, and Riverside. Check predicted eruption times at the visitor center; **Grand Geyser** is far more impressive than Old Faithful if you can time it.
- **Grand Prismatic Overlook** via the Fairy Falls trail (3.2 mi round trip) — the *only* way to actually see the colors of the largest hot spring in the country. The boardwalk at ground level shows you steam.
- **Midway and Lower Geyser Basins**, then south past West Thumb.

Continue over the south entrance into **Grand Teton** and on to Jackson (allow 3h30 total with stops, more with a bison jam). Nights 2–4.

### Day 3 — Mon Sep 21 · Cascade Canyon
**Jenny Lake shuttle boat → Hidden Falls → Inspiration Point → Cascade Canyon.** With the boat it's about 4.5 mi round trip to the canyon mouth, or 9+ mi if you walk the lake and push up-canyon. This is the classic Teton day hike: you're walking into a glacial U between 12,000-ft walls, with moose in the willows.

**Verify the boat's last operating day** — it typically stops in late September, adding 2.4 mi each way if you miss it.

### Day 4 — Tue Sep 22 · Delta Lake or Amphitheater Lake
The hard day. Both start at Lupine Meadows:
- **Delta Lake** — 8 mi, 2,300 ft, with an unmaintained boulder-field scramble at the end. The payoff is a glacial lake the color of antifreeze directly beneath the Grand. Best photograph in the park.
- **Amphitheater Lake** — 9.6 mi, 3,000 ft, all maintained switchbacks. Harder aerobically, easier technically.

Dawn first: **Oxbow Bend** and **Schwabacher Landing** for the Snake River reflections with aspens turning. Then **Mormon Row** for the barns.

Evening in Jackson — Snake River Brewing, the Silver Dollar, or a proper dinner at Glorietta or Snake River Grill.

### Day 5 — Wed Sep 23 · North through the park
Drive back into Yellowstone via the south entrance. Long, slow, stop-constantly day:
- **Hayden Valley** — bison herds, and the best odds of wolves at dawn or dusk
- **Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone** — Artist Point, Lookout Point, and **Uncle Tom's / Brink of the Lower Falls** stairs for the vertical version
- **Mount Washburn** (6.4 mi, 1,400 ft) if you want one more summit — bighorn sheep and a 360° view over the whole caldera

Night 5 at **Canyon Lodge** or **Lake Yellowstone Hotel** (verify closing dates — Lake typically closes early October).

### Day 6 — Thu Sep 24 · Lamar Valley and out to Paradise
Pre-dawn to **Lamar Valley** — the "Serengeti of North America" and the best wolf-watching in the lower 48. Bring binoculars or a spotting scope; look for the cluster of parked cars with tripods, and talk to those people, they're generous with sightings.

Then **Mammoth Hot Springs** — travertine terraces, and elk lying on the lawns of the old fort buildings, bugling. In rut week the bulls are genuinely dangerous; stay 25 yards back, more if a bull is herding.

Exit north through Gardiner to **Paradise Valley**. Nights 6–7.

### Day 7 — Fri Sep 25 · Do nothing, on purpose
**Chico Hot Springs** — an 1900 lodge with two open-air mineral pools, no phones out, mountains on all sides. Soak in the morning. The **dining room at Chico** is the best restaurant within 50 miles and worth dressing up slightly for; order the flaming orange dessert.

If anyone still wants to move: the **Yellowstone River** through Paradise Valley is world-class trout water and late September is prime — a half-day guided float is easy to arrange out of Livingston. Or drive **Emigrant Peak / Pine Creek Falls** for a short walk in the Absarokas.

### Day 8 — Sat Sep 26 · Home
Chico → BZN is 1h15. If there's time, **Livingston** is a genuinely good main street for a last coffee.

## Lodging

**In-park (Days 5)** — [Canyon Lodge](https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/), [Lake Yellowstone Hotel](https://www.yellowstonenationalparklodges.com/), or Old Faithful Inn. These book **12–13 months ahead**; late September sometimes has cancellations, and it's worth checking weekly. If nothing opens up, **Gardiner** or **West Yellowstone** motels are the fallback and cost half as much.

**Jackson (3 nights)** — expensive. [Hotel Jackson](https://www.hoteljackson.com/) and the [Anvil](https://www.anvilhotel.com/) downtown; [Teton Village](https://www.jacksonhole.com/) is quieter and closer to trailheads. Budget **$350–600/night**, less after the September rate drop.

**Paradise Valley (2 nights)** — [Chico Hot Springs](https://chicohotsprings.com/) is the whole point. Book the main lodge for character or a cabin for space. **$200–350/night**.

1. **Confirm the Delta AUS→BZN Saturday nonstop still runs Sep 19 and Sep 26.** Everything else depends on it.

3. Rental SUV out of BZN.
4. Chico Hot Springs.
5. In-park night (waitlist / check for cancellations).

## Risks

- **Early snow.** Dunraven Pass (Mount Washburn) and the Beartooth close with the first serious storm. Everything else stays open; you just drive it in snow.
- **Distance.** This is ~900 miles of driving in a week, and Yellowstone's speed limit is 45 mph with regular bison-caused standstills. It is not a relaxing week until Day 7 — which is the design, but go in knowing it.
- **Altitude.** Jackson is 6,200 ft, the Teton trailheads start at 6,700 ft, and Delta Lake tops out near 9,000 ft. Coming from Austin at 500 ft, give yourself Day 1 and 2 to adjust before the big hikes — which the itinerary does deliberately.
- **Wildlife distance rules** are legal requirements, not suggestions: 25 yards from elk and bison, 100 yards from bears and wolves. Carry bear spray on every hike, and know how to use it.
