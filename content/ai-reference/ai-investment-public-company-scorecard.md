This is investment research, not financial advice. The page is a scorecard, not a recommendation list. Multiples, backlogs, and estimate-revision directions are pulled as of 2026-11-05 ± 1 month and will move with prints, guidance updates, and tape action. The structural columns — bucket, bottleneck controlled, AI/DC revenue exposure, customer concentration — are more durable than the valuation columns and are the better lens for thesis durability. Cells marked `n/d` are not disclosed by the company at the level of granularity required; cells marked `n/a` are not a meaningful metric for that name (typical of pre-EBITDA neoclouds and miners with HPC pivots). This cycle's refresh found unusually wide dispersion across secondary valuation aggregators (StockAnalysis, GuruFocus, Seeking Alpha, Yahoo) for several names — those multiples are tagged `^[ambiguous]` rather than asserted with false precision; a handful of names (SO, CEG, VST, TLN, EQT, CIFR, HUT, BTDR) had not yet reported their most recent quarter, or that quarter could not be confirmed, as of this refresh.

## How to read this scorecard

- **Bottleneck controlled** is the single most important column. A name with no scarce asset is just an AI narrative.
- **Estimate-revision direction (12m)** matters more than the absolute multiple. A 30x P/E with rising estimates compresses faster than a 15x P/E with falling estimates. `↑` = consensus 2026/2027 EPS revised up over the trailing 12 months; `→` = flat; `↓` = down.
- **Purity discounts the multiple needed**. A pure-play (Vertiv, Modine data center segment, neoclouds) commands a higher multiple before it is "expensive" than a diversified industrial with 10% data center exposure.
- **Backlog or book-to-bill** > 1.0 with sequential growth is the cleanest leading indicator the bottleneck thesis is working. Watch sequential change, not just absolute level.

## Highest-conviction by bucket

- **Electrical**: ETN and SU.PA — only platforms able to deliver complete prefab electrical systems at hyperscale; backlog growth confirms, and ETN's pending COL Group acquisition adds European reach. POWL is the small-cap pure expression, now at 3.0x book-to-bill.
- **Power gen**: GEV — gas turbine slots remain the binding constraint, backlog hit a record $197B, but slot-reservation growth decelerated sharply this quarter (+6 GW vs. double-digit GW/qtr in 1H'26); watch the next print before treating this as a trend. ENR.DE is the European pair-trade with comparable Grid Tech profile.
- **Memory**: MU and 000660.KS — HBM/DRAM essentially sold out 2026-27; structural under-supply continues. Multiples have actually compressed further this cycle (MU ~5x fwd P/E) even as earnings accelerated — the cyclical-anchoring discount has widened, not narrowed.
- **Semis/equip**: ASML (EUV monopoly, contract liabilities nearly tripled QoQ as a backlog proxy) and BESI (hybrid bonding pure-play, though HBM adoption of hybrid bonding reportedly pushed back at memory makers). NVDA's forward multiple compressed from ~30x to ~17x even as DC revenue hit $89B — re-rating, not de-risking.
- **Optics/networking**: COHR (vertically integrated, FY27 "essentially booked" into CY28) and ANET (only credible Ethernet alternative to Nvidia networking at scale). CRDO sold off on its print despite a beat — the first sign of optics-bucket estimate friction.
- **Cooling**: VRT (still the largest liquid-cooling pure play, though it stopped disclosing quarterly backlog/book-to-bill after FY2025 — a disclosure-regime change worth flagging) and MOD (new >$4B multi-year hyperscaler cooling LTA).
- **EPC**: PWR ($63.7B record backlog) and EME (cleanest DC backlog disclosure at 47% of RPO, though the stock sold off 10% on hyperscaler-concentration concerns raised by analysts).
- **Neocloud / power-first**: NBIS (cleanest balance sheet, net cash, $37.5B RPO) is now the clear top pick in the bucket. **CRWV's credit profile deteriorated sharply this cycle** — 5y CDS blew through 1,000bps in October 2026 — and the CoreWeave–Core Scientific merger was terminated by shareholder vote. APLD has moved to de-risk its CRWV exposure by restructuring leases onto a higher-rated subsidiary. Treat CRWV-adjacent names (APLD, CORZ) as elevated-risk until the credit situation stabilizes.
- **Utilities/IPPs**: CEG and TLN — nuclear PPAs remain the scarcest power product hyperscalers can buy, and CEG has diversified its PPA book (Google, Amazon, Meta, Walmart) beyond the original MSFT anchor. Dominion's new VA large-load tariff and AEP's reversal back to 63-69 GW of incremental contracted load (from a prior 13 GW cut) are the two most consequential regulatory swings this cycle.
- **Second-order**: FCX (copper deficit thesis, though 2027-28 EBITDA sensitivity was trimmed), KMI (gas pipeline backlog now >75% power/DC-linked per analyst estimates, up from ~60%).

## Caveats and disclosures

Multiples shown are NTM consensus where extractable, otherwise approximated from public commentary; they will move with every print and tape close. This cycle's refresh found unusually wide (2-3x) dispersion across secondary valuation aggregators for many names — those cells are tagged `^[ambiguous]` rather than asserted with false precision, and a live terminal (FactSet/Bloomberg/Visible Alpha) pull is recommended before treating any single multiple as authoritative. Backlogs are reported with a one-quarter lag and definitions vary (RPOs vs orders vs reservation agreements vs slot reservations are not directly comparable); Vertiv notably stopped disclosing quarterly backlog/book-to-bill after FY2025, a disclosure-regime change rather than a data gap. 5-yr median multiples are eyeballed from public charts and labelled `n/m` for post-IPO or post-spin names where the history is too short. Customer concentration is rarely disclosed at top-1; many entries are inferred from SEC filings and conference commentary and labelled `^[ambiguous]`. Net leverage is approximated from the most recent 10-Q and excludes operating leases. A handful of names (SO, CEG, VST, TLN, EQT) had not yet reported their most recent quarter as of this refresh date, and CIFR/HUT/BTDR's most recent quarterly actuals could not be confirmed from a primary source — those rows carry forward the last confirmed data with `^[ambiguous]` flags rather than invented figures. Private-company peers (Schneider's private competitors in DCIM, private cooling specialists, private gas-turbine packagers, private contract miners) are out of scope. This is investment research, not a recommendation; do your own work.
